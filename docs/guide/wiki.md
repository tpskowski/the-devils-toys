# The wiki

[← Back to the guide](README.md)

The **Wiki** is the room's notebook: pages and folders for the facts a table
discovers, the places it has been, and the notes each player wants to keep. It
appears as a **Wiki** tab in the room's main panel once the GM has turned it on.
The same notebook is also available from **Refs** and Room Config.

It is not the rulebook, and it is not a second Library. The Library holds the
files a room owns; the Wiki is written Markdown that can point at those files
and at other things in the room.

## Your notebook

Start a page with **New page** and sort pages into folders from the left-hand
tree. A page and folder you create are yours: other players cannot edit, move,
or delete them. The GM can work anywhere in the Wiki, as they can in the rest of
the room.

New pages are **private**. Only you and the GM can find them, open them, or find
them in search; a private page is not merely hidden in the browser. Folders are
private to manage too, although a folder on a route to a shared page can appear
so that shared page still has a usable path.

The GM may choose **Share** on a finished page. That makes the entire page
readable by the room. Players cannot share or unshare pages, including their own
notes. A shared page remains editable by its owner and the GM; sharing does not
turn it into a document every player can change.

## Writing and linking

The editor is a normal rich Markdown editor, with **Use plain Markdown** as a
fallback when a browser cannot load it. Type `@` while writing to choose a
visible room entry. The resulting chip can name:

- a character, NPC, follower, Library asset, room item, or another Wiki page;
- the thing's current title, rather than a number you have to remember.

Click a page chip to open that page. A character, follower, or asset chip opens
the same room surface you would normally use; NPC and item chips identify the
thing without exposing a second detail view.

The picker only offers things you may already read. If a page you can read names
something that later becomes hidden from you, the page still says its written
label, but it stops being a chip. In particular, an NPC must be revealed before
a player can mention or follow it. A shared page is not a loophole around an
unrevealed NPC, a hidden handout, or another player's private page.

## When two people write at once

Saving checks the version you began with. If somebody saved the same page first,
the Wiki shows the current page and your draft side by side. Choose **Use current
page** to discard your draft, or **Keep my draft** to put it back in the editor
against the newer page; read and combine the two before saving. Nobody's writing
is silently overwritten.

## Map legends

A GM can bind one Wiki page as a **legend** for a map in **Room Config →
Library**, or choose **Write one** there. When that map is open, its legend
button opens the page beside it.

One map has at most one legend and one page can belong to at most one map. A
legend obeys the page's sharing setting: a visible map with a private legend
does not reveal the legend. Share the page when the table is meant to read it;
unsharing it closes it for players without hiding or changing the map itself.
