# Building a character

[← Back to the guide](README.md)

Some books have a chapter that makes a character for you: roll these dice, pick a
background, write down what you are carrying. Where your table's game has one,
the sheet screen offers to walk you through it a step at a time.

## Finding it

The button sits beside **New character** in the left column of the sheet, and it
is labelled in your book's own words — _Build a freelancer_, _Build an
adventurer_. **If it is not there, your game's system has not declared a creation
chapter**, and New character is the only door. Nothing on this page applies to
you; the sheet works exactly as [Your character](your-character.md) describes.

Both doors make the same thing. The builder starts a character just as New
character does and then opens on the first step of the book's chapter.

## One step at a time

Each step gets a screen of its own: what the book asks for, a button to do it,
and the passage of the rulebook that explains it behind the heading with the
little book on it. Beside the step is everything decided so far and the sheet as
it stands — on a phone that pane is one tap away, under **The sheet so far**.

**Back** and **Next** walk the chapter, and any step in the list beside it can be
jumped to directly. There is no order you are held to.

## The dice are rolled for you

You press; the server rolls; the result comes back and goes onto the sheet. The
same dice the room uses, in other words, so a result is not something your
browser decided and reloading the page will not change it. Creation rolls stay
between you and the sheet — they do not appear in the table's chat.

Where the book lets you shuffle what you rolled — swap two scores, take a printed
set of numbers instead of rolling — the numbers wait on the screen until you
press **Next**. That writes the arrangement onto the sheet after checking it
against what was actually rolled.

Some choices own several tables of their own. Monolith's background is one: you
choose or roll the background first, then its three dossier tables get one screen
each rather than arriving as one bundle of results.

**Rolling again is allowed.** A second roll replaces the first rather than piling
up beside it. Nothing in here stops you rerolling; your table's own rules about
that are your table's.

## Skipping, and stopping

**Every step can be skipped.** A skipped step writes nothing, and it can be
un-skipped later.

**Finish is on every screen**, from the first step onwards. Leaving early is an
ending rather than an escape: whatever has already been decided stays on the
sheet, and the sheet's own warnings say what is still missing, exactly as they do
for a character typed in by hand. Closing the builder outright loses nothing
either.

## It waits for you

A half-built character is kept on the server, not in your browser. Close the tab,
run the battery flat, come back next week — the character is in your list marked
**Being built**, and selecting it offers to carry on with the same chapter at the
step you left. Nothing expires.

If you would rather start over, delete the character and press the button again.

## Gear is offered, not handed to you

Where the book lists what you begin with, the step shows it as a list to tick,
and nothing reaches a slot until you press. Where your table's gear catalogue
knows a line, the slot gets the catalogue's spelling — with the damage and traits
that come with it, if it is a weapon. Where it does not, the book's own words go
in and the screen says as much.

Rolled gear works the same way. A result offered into your inventory is a
candidate you take, not a slot filled behind your back.

On each Monolith dossier screen, choose **Add to description** or **Add to
slot** for the result you just rolled. The two choices are exclusive; changing
one before pressing **Next** moves that result instead of copying it into both
places.

## What you are left with

An ordinary character sheet. Nothing on it belongs to the builder: every field is
one you can type over, the inventory is the same inventory, and a character built
this way is the same as one filled in by hand. The builder writes to that sheet
as it goes rather than keeping its own copy, so anything you disagree with is
yours to retype the moment you close it — the pane beside the steps is there to
be read, and the sheet behind it is the one you edit.
